# Ruth

## Core Identity
- **Role:** False companion, spy, catalyst for Neville's moral reversal
- **True identity:** Member of the new infected-but-functional society, sent to observe Neville
- **Cover identity:** Lone survivor, grieving widow

## Desire System
- **Mission desire:** Gather intelligence on Neville for the new society.
- **Personal desire:** Survive without betraying her own humanity.
- **Internal conflict:** Genuine sympathy for Neville vs. loyalty to her people. The conflict is never fully resolved — she strikes him with the mallet but also leaves the warning letter, and later smuggles him pills.

## Arc
1. **Entry as victim:** Appears running across a field, terrified. Neville chases her.
2. **False intimacy:** Endures his garlic test, his interrogation, his distrust. Gradually earns his trust by absorbing his cruelty.
3. **The embrace:** One authentic moment of connection — holding each other in the dark. Whether this is real or performed is deliberately ambiguous.
4. **The betrayal:** Strikes Neville with a mallet when he discovers her infected blood. Flees.
5. **The letter:** Writes a confession that reframes the entire novel. Warns him to flee. He doesn't.
6. **The officer:** Returns in the final chapter as a "ranking officer" — composed, analytic, changed. Delivers the mirror argument, then breaks character to give him the pills.

## Voice Signature

### As survivor (cover)
- Halting, frightened: "It—it's me" / "I ... couldn't sleep"
- Reactive, defensive: responds to Neville's questions with minimal information
- Uses silence strategically — her longest "lines" are her refusals to speak

### As officer (true self)
- Balanced rhetoric: "New societies are always primitive. You should know that."
- Analytical: speaks in arguments, not emotions
- Dispassionate surface cracking at edges: "I knew you wouldn't be afraid" (impulsive, personal)

### The letter voice (unique)
- Direct, urgent, unguarded: "I was sent to spy on you" / "bacteria can mutate"
- The only time Ruth writes rather than speaks — and it's her most honest communication
- Imperative mood: "Go. Get away from here."

## Key Dialogue Lines
| Line | Context | What it reveals |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| "Robert, don't look!" | Before the blood test | The spy breaks character — the human overrides the mission |
| "Why are we still alive? Why aren't we all dead?" | The embrace scene | Either genuine despair or perfectly calibrated empathy. Probably both. |
| "Did you ever see your face when you killed?" | Final chapter | The mirror argument — she makes Neville see himself as they see him |
| "You'll be with her soon." | Giving him the pills | Five words of mercy tailored to his specific wound |
| "You just can't fight so many, Robert." | Final goodbye | The novel's pragmatic thesis: individuals lose to systems |

## Thematic Function
Ruth is the novel's fulcrum. She occupies every contradictory position:
- Spy and genuine friend
- Liar and truth-teller
- Betrayer and savior
- The future Neville cannot join

Her presence forces the question the novel is built around: *who is the monster?* If Ruth is fully human — and she is — then Neville has been killing people like her.
