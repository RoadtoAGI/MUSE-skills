# Robert Neville

## Core Identity
- **Role:** Protagonist, sole survivor (as he believes), last human
- **Age:** Mid-30s at plague onset
- **Background:** Factory worker, WWII veteran (Panama), married father. Not educated — self-taught in science after the plague.

## Desire System
- **Conscious desire:** Survive. Understand the plague. Find a cure.
- **Unconscious desire:** Reconnect with Virginia. Be absolved of what he did to her body.
- **Need (what would actually save him):** Accept that the world has moved on without him. Adapt or join the new order.
- **Fatal flaw:** Cannot release the past. Cannot trust. The same stubbornness that keeps him alive keeps him from adapting.

## Arc
1. **Baseline:** Functional alcoholic in a fortified house. Routine as religion.
2. **Scientific awakening:** Discovers the bacillus, develops garlic/cross/mirror theories. Brief sense of purpose.
3. **Ruth encounter:** Opens up for the first time in three years. Immediately punished.
4. **Recognition:** In the death cell, he finally sees himself through their eyes. "I'm the abnormal one now."
5. **Transcendence:** Accepts his role as legend — the monster of the new world's mythology. Dies with a "coughing chuckle."

## Voice Signature

### Vocabulary
- Blue-collar diction mixed with self-taught scientific terminology
- "vampiris," "bacillus," "phagocytic cells" — proud of the words, uses them with slight formality
- Swears plainly: "bastard," "goddamn," "the hell"
- Self-addresses in second person when under stress: "You have a mind, don't you?"

### Rhythm
- Short, punchy sentences in action/stress: "Enough!" / "Oh, God, the keys!"
- Longer, more introspective sentences in isolation: meditative, circular, often interrupted by self-contradiction
- The split-mind voice: sarcastic observer vs. panicked self, conducting internal arguments

### Emotional tells
- Reaches for whisky at every emotional crisis — the glass is his punctuation mark
- Body symptoms narrated before emotions are named: "knotting heat," "palsied legs," "teeth grating"
- Virginia's name is his involuntary cry — it surfaces at moments of total collapse, never by choice

## Key Dialogue Lines

## Relationships
- **Virginia:** The irrecoverable past. Every woman he meets is measured against her ghost.
- **Ben Cortman:** Dark double. Both are trapped in compulsive repetition. Neville mourns Cortman's death.
- **Ruth:** The future he couldn't trust. Their one night of genuine connection is framed by espionage.
- **The dog (Ch. 12–13):** Neville's attempt to save the stray dog, which dies anyway, is a rehearsal for his failure with Ruth.
